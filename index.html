What is 5WORDS?

5WORDS is a daily word puzzle built around a single 5×5 grid. Each day the game takes five hidden five-letter words, shuffles all twenty-five of their letters across the board, and leaves you to put them back.

Where most word games ask you to guess a word, 5WORDS gives you every letter up front. Nothing is hidden and nothing is random — the answer is already on the screen, just in the wrong order. That makes it less a game of vocabulary recall and more a game of deduction and rearrangement.

Your score is the number of times you press CHECK, so the challenge is not just solving the board but solving it efficiently. Everyone plays the same puzzle each day, and your streak and best score are kept on your own device.

How it works

  1. Swap any two letters

    Tap one letter, then tap another, and the two trade places. Letters move anywhere on the board — across rows and columns alike.

  2. Press CHECK when you're ready

    The board colours itself in to show how close you are. Every press counts towards your score, so make as many swaps as you dare before checking.

  3. Solve all five rows

    When all twenty-five tiles turn green, every row spells a valid five-letter word and the puzzle is done. Then compare your check count with friends.

What the colours mean

  • Green — right letter, right square. Leave it alone.

  • Orange — this letter belongs in this row, but in a different column.

  • Pink — this letter belongs in this column, but in a different row.

  • No colour — this letter is in the wrong row and the wrong column.

Strategy tips

  • Look for common endings first — ‑ING, ‑ATE, ‑ION, ‑EST. They lock several letters at once.
  • Count your vowels before you move anything. Five five-letter words usually need a vowel or two per row, so a row holding four vowels is telling you something.
  • Batch your swaps. Since your score is the number of checks, ten swaps then one check always beats one swap then one check.
  • Treat green as frozen. Once a tile is green, every later swap should route around it.
  • Use pink tiles as anchors. A pink letter tells you the column is right, so you only need to work out which of the five rows it belongs to.
  • Remember that every swap moves two letters. Fixing one row will often break another, so plan a chain of swaps rather than one at a time.

Why play a daily word puzzle?

A few minutes of word play is a genuinely pleasant way to start the day, and 5WORDS leans on a slightly different set of skills than a guessing game does:

  • Pattern recognition — spotting a word inside a mess of letters.
  • Working memory — holding a planned sequence of swaps in your head.
  • Deductive reasoning — narrowing down positions from the colour feedback.
  • Vocabulary — five new five-letter words every single day.

And because there is exactly one puzzle a day, it finishes. No endless scroll, no next level — just a few minutes, a streak to keep, and a score to beat tomorrow.

Similar games you might like

If you enjoy 5WORDS, these daily word games scratch a similar itch. We have noted how each one differs, so you can find the flavour of puzzle you are after.

  • Waffle

    The closest cousin to 5WORDS. You drag letters around a waffle-shaped grid to form six interlocking words, with a budget of fifteen swaps and stars awarded for every swap you have left over.

    Different because: words run across and down, and you are limited to a fixed number of swaps rather than an unlimited number of checks.

    Play Waffle

  • Wordle

    The game that started the daily-puzzle boom. Guess a hidden five-letter word in six attempts, with tiles turning green and yellow to show which letters you have found.

    Different because: the letters are hidden, so it is a game of guessing and elimination. In 5WORDS you are given every letter from the start.

    Play Wordle

  • Connections

    Sixteen words, four secret categories. Sort them into the right groups of four with only four mistakes allowed, dodging the overlaps planted to catch you out.

    Different because: it is about meaning and association rather than spelling or letter positions.

    Play Connections

  • Spelling Bee

    Seven letters in a honeycomb. Build as many words as you can, always using the centre letter, and chase the pangram that uses all seven.

    Different because: it is open-ended and scored on volume — there is no single solution to reach.

    Play Spelling Bee

  • Quordle

    Four Wordle grids at once. Every guess you type is played against all four boards simultaneously, and you get nine attempts to crack the lot.

    Different because: it is Wordle scaled up — the difficulty comes from juggling four puzzles, not from rearranging a board.

    Play Quordle

  • Squaredle

    A grid of letters in which you trace words through adjacent tiles, Boggle-style, hunting for every word hidden in the layout plus a set of bonus finds.

    Different because: the grid never changes shape — you are searching a fixed board rather than rebuilding one.

    Play Squaredle
